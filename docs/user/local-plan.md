# Making a local plan, and consulting the public

*Everyone who can see a project can see this page. Planners and district administrators record the steps, consultations and comments. The approval is the district administrator's to record.*

A project's map and data are the working material of a local plan. This page keeps the **procedure** of making that plan: the steps the Regulations set and their time limits (L.I. 2384 r.23–27), the consultations held (r.65), and the public's comments on the draft. Open a project and choose **Local plan**.

## About the plan
- **Prepared by:** the District Spatial Planning Committee, or another person (a developer or a consultant for an estate, say) who then applies for the Assembly's approval. This changes the steps: see below.
- **Structure plan in force since:** the Regulations have the local plan directed within **twelve months** of the structure plan coming into force (r.23(1)).
- **Plan valid until** and **conditions imposed:** what the Committee may attach to a local plan under r.27.

## Steps and deadlines
Each step shows the rule it comes from, the Schedule form of its notice where there is one, the date it is due, and the date it was done. Press **Done today** when a step is taken. A step done after its date is marked late; one not done by its date is shown in red.

| Step | Time | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The planning authority directs the Committee to prepare the plan | 12 months from the structure plan | r.23(1) |
| Notice of commencement published (Form 27) | | r.23(2) |
| Draft local plan completed | | r.23(3) |
| Draft opened for inspection, and the public invited to comment (Form 28) | | r.23(3)–(5) |
| Local plan approved | 90 days from the application, for a plan prepared by another person | r.25(1) |
| Notice of approval published and displayed (Form 29) | 7 days from the approval | r.25(3)–(5) |

For a plan **prepared by another person**, three more steps follow (r.26): submission to the Regional Spatial Planning Committee for confirmation (Form 30), its confirmation within **28 days**, and the notice of confirmation within **7 days** (Form 31).

Steps follow one another: the draft cannot be put out before it is complete, and **the plan cannot be approved until the public's sixty days are over**.

Download the notices of commencement, of the draft open for comment, and of approval from this page.

## What the plan specifies
Regulation 24 lists seventeen things a local plan specifies, from population density to drainage and waste. Tick each one the plan covers and note where (or why not). The count shows at a glance what is still missing before the draft goes out.

## Consultations
Record each public or stakeholder consultation: when and where it was held, who was consulted, how many attended, the issues raised, and what was agreed. **Download the minutes**, and **Display in the Public Data Room**: the Regulations require a copy of the minutes to be displayed there (r.65(3)).

## Comments on the draft
From the day the draft is opened for inspection, the public has **sixty days** to comment (r.23(5)).

- **Online:** if the project is published in the [Public Data Room](public-data-room.md), the plan is listed there with the closing date and the minutes on display, and anyone can send a comment without an account. They can say which part of the plan it is about, as the Regulations ask (r.23(6)).
- **At the office:** record a comment received on paper (Form 59) with **Record a comment**.

Each comment shows whether it has been answered. Record the Committee's response to each; the page counts those still unanswered. Names and contact details are shown only to those who may see personal data, and are never public.

## What the public sees
For a published project, once the notice of commencement or of the draft is recorded: the plan's name and locality, where it has got to, the last day for comments, the minutes put on display, and, once approved, the date of approval.

## Facilities against the planning standards
The Revised Zoning Guidelines and Planning Standards (LUSPA, 2025) give, for each kind of facility, how many people one serves and, for schools and water, how far a home may be from the nearest. The section **Facilities against the planning standards** checks the planning area against them, for what a local plan specifies under r.24(1)(b).

1. Say which layer holds the facilities, and its **type** field, under Relationships, Layers (role *Infrastructure and facilities*). The *Places (OpenStreetMap)* layer works too, with *amenity* as the type field.
2. Give the **population** of the planning area, or let it come from a population layer, or from the number of buildings (five people each by default).

For each facility the table shows the standard, how many are needed, how many are on the map, how many buildings are beyond the walking distance, and whether the area falls short:

| Facility | Standard (section) |
| --- | --- |
| Primary school | 1 per 5,000 people, within 2.5 km (3.1.1.2(b)) |
| Junior High School | 1 per 10,000, within 3.2 km (3.1.1.2(c)) |
| Nursery or kindergarten | within 1.25 km (3.1.1.2(a)) |
| Senior High or Technical School | 1 per 20,000 (3.2) |
| CHPS compound; health centre or clinic; polyclinic; district hospital | 1 per 5,000; 15,000; 50,000; 200,000 (5.9) |
| Local market; neighbourhood shopping centre | 1 per 30,000; 15,000 (6.5) |
| Local sports field | 1 per 5,000, 1 acre per 1,000 people (7.1.1.2) |
| Park or public garden | 1.2 acres per 1,000 people (7.1.2.1) |
| Borehole; public standpipe | 1 per 300; 1 per 100, within 400 m (13.3) |
| Waste collection point | 1 per 1,000, within 500 m (13.2.3.1) |
| Fire station or post | 1 per 35,000 (14.3.2) |

Facilities are recognised by words in their type (*school*, *JHS*, *CHPS*, *clinic*, *market*, *borehole*…). Those not recognised are listed so their type can be corrected.
