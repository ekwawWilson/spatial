# The Public Data Room

*District administrators open and manage it; planners answer requests. The public needs no account.*

The Act requires every Assembly to keep a Public Data Room where the public can see spatial plans, permits and notices (Act 925 s.47; L.I. 2384 r.64), and a register of them that is also on the Assembly's website (r.67). The platform puts the room online. In the menu, choose **Public Data Room**.

Nothing is public until you open the room and publish a project.

## Opening the room
1. **Open the room online.** The public page is then at `/public/<your district's code>`, for example `/public/SMA`. Give that address out, link to it from the Assembly's website, or print it on notices.
2. Write a **welcome** and **the office** details: where the Physical Planning Department is, its hours, phone and email.
3. Decide whether the register **names the people permits were issued to**. Names are personal data (r.68(2)(b)): leave this off unless the Assembly has decided to publish them. Phone numbers, emails and addresses are never shown, whatever you choose.

**Close the room online** takes everything down again at once.

## Publishing a project
Under **Published projects**, choose a project and **Publish**. Two things then become public, and you can untick either:

- **Permits in the register:** the project's applications appear in the public register.
- **Zoning lookup:** the project's zoning answers "What can I build here?".

## What the public sees
| On the public page | What it shows | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **What can I build here?** | For a place (their location, or coordinates): the zone, the uses it permits and prohibits, and the Assembly's site standards for it | The zoning layer (or the parcel's land use) of published projects, the national zones, and the district's adopted standards |
| **Check an application** | From an application number: its status, when it was received, and the next step with its date | The application itself |
| **Register of permits** | Permits issued (with their validity), applications pending, and applications refused with the grounds | Applications in published projects (r.67(3)) |
| **Plans and notices** | Documents on display, and the approved zoning schemes | What you put on display; the zoning scheme register |
| **Permit fees** | The fees entered under Permits | The fee-fixing resolution's lines |
| **Ask for information** | A form: the person gets a reference and the date by which the Assembly answers | See below |

Withdrawn applications are not in the register. The register is put together from the records each time someone looks, so it is never out of date.

## Documents on display
Under **Documents on display**, add plans, notices and records of public consultation (r.64(2)). Give each a title and, where it applies, the last day it is on display: it comes down by itself. A draft local plan is on display for 60 days (r.23).

## Requests for information
A member of the public fills in **Ask for information** on the public page (in place of Form 55), with a phone number or an email address. They are given a **reference** and the date an answer is due.

The Regulations give the Assembly **five days** (r.64(7)) and ask for a record of every request (r.64(8)). Under **Requests for information** you see each request, when it is due, and those past their five days in red. Reach the person, then **Record the answer** you gave, or **Decline, with this reason**. The record is kept.
